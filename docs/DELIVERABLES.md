# Challenge Deliverables

> **Status note (2026-09-21):** this file is a snapshot of the challenge deliverables as of ADR-011, when `exp0026` was the canonical baseline. The canonical has since changed to `exp0038_clock_heading` (ADR-015), a 46-input gait-clock policy with a different reward and a seven-run training lineage. **`IMPLEMENTATION.md` is the current, maintained writeup** (formulas, training recipe, evaluation, reproduction, limits); read this file for the history of how the earlier baselines were reached.

## Approach writeup
**Goal**: get a Unitree G1 walking (or clearly attempting to) in simulation, with an understood, working, end-to-end RL pipeline -- prioritized over gait polish, per the challenge document.

**Architecture decision**: after auditing this machine (Apple M5 Pro, macOS, no NVIDIA GPU, 15 CPU cores, 24GB RAM) and re-verifying the current state of the official Unitree repos, we chose **direct MuJoCo + Gymnasium + stable-baselines3 (PPO)** over the challenge-linked `unitree_rl_gym` (trains via Isaac Gym, Nvidia+Linux only) and `unitree_rl_mjlab` (trains via MuJoCo Warp, Nvidia GPU required, macOS is evaluation-only per its own docs). Full reasoning and sources: `docs/DECISIONS.md` ADR-001, `docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md` R-0001/R-0003.

**Model**: Unitree G1 (`g1_29dof_rev_1_0`), vendored MJCF from `mujoco_menagerie`. We RL-control only the 12 leg joints (hip x3/knee/ankle x2, per leg), converting them from MuJoCo's built-in `<position>` actuators to `<motor>` (direct torque) so a PD controller we own maps policy position-targets to torque -- matching the challenge's "target joint positions as outputs." Waist and arms are held at the model's own upstream "stand" pose and are not RL-controlled (`docs/DECISIONS.md` ADR-002).

**Pipeline**: model load -> physics stepping -> PD control mapping -> Gymnasium env (reset/step/observation/reward/termination) -> stable-baselines3 PPO (rollout collection -> GAE/value/clipped update, all owned by the library) -> checkpoint -> deterministic evaluation. Every stage was smoke-tested in isolation before the next was built (`experiments/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` EXP-0001 through EXP-0004) -- see docs/SIMULATION.md and docs/RL_PIPELINE.md for the full implemented contract.

**Result**: a first bounded, CPU-only training run (3M timesteps, ~7 minutes wall-clock on 12 parallel envs, `baseline0001`) took mean episode survival from ~1.3s (untrained, falls immediately regardless of command) to a deterministic-eval mean of 16.5s, with 15/20 fixed-seed episodes surviving the full 20s episode and a mean **1.85m of real forward/lateral displacement per episode (per-episode ratio to commanded distance: 0.479)** -- genuine, if imperfect, walking, not reward-hacking. We then iterated through twelve more experiments (`experiments/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` EXP-0006 through EXP-0017, `docs/FAILURE_ANALYSIS.md` F-0002 through F-0004, `docs/DECISIONS.md` ADR-003 through ADR-008):

1. Three reward/PPO-config changes (`target_kl` alone, lower `alive_bonus` alone, both combined) each made things worse or found a degenerate "stand nearly still" policy that only looked better by reward/survival until we checked *distance traveled*.
2. What actually fixed the residual falls was simply training longer at the *original, unmodified config* -- extending to 10M timesteps revealed the reward curve oscillates in a repeating climb-crash-recover pattern that trends upward over many cycles; every checkpoint from 4M timesteps onward beat the 3M-step baseline.
3. A user-reported concern ("does it walk straight or take steps?") led to a new diagnostic (`diagnostics/gait_diagnostic.py`) that found the resulting policy drifted **95 degrees in 10 seconds** under a pure-forward command and took tiny, chattery steps -- invisible to every metric used so far, since velocity tracking is computed in the robot's own rotating frame. A first fix attempt overcorrected into the same degenerate stillness as step 1 (caught immediately by the same distance-traveled check); measuring the actual gait's own yaw-rate cost before recalibrating the penalty weight fixed the heading half of the problem for real.
4. The stepping-quality half proved harder: two from-scratch attempts to reward longer swing phases both failed (one got gamed into a skating gait that avoids lifting its feet at all; the fix for that loophole caused a 100% fall rate) -- both are documented negative results, not discarded. What actually worked was **fine-tuning from the already-good checkpoint at a low learning rate**, instead of training the new reward term from scratch: an established policy has real, working reward to lose by switching to skating, unlike one that's still randomly exploring. Median swing duration doubled (20ms -> 40ms, directly measured). Continuing to fine-tune the same lineage for 10M more timesteps pushed every metric further -- **0/20 falls, the first perfect score in the project** -- though swing duration itself plateaued at the doubled value, separating "policy still improving" from "stride duration still increasing."

That work (EXP-0006 through EXP-0020) produced a strong baseline (`exp0017_warmstart_air_time_long`) but also characterized what looked like a fundamental 3-way trade-off between tracking precision, stride length, and heading control (`docs/DECISIONS.md` ADR-009/ADR-010) -- no checkpoint matched the best of all three at once. A follow-up investigation (EXP-0022 through EXP-0026) resolved this. Testing whether the trade-off was caused by the *sequential fine-tuning* method itself (rather than a property of the reward) led first to two informative failures -- training the full multi-term reward from scratch collapsed to a "stand still and collect the survival bonus" degenerate optimum regardless of learning rate (EXP-0022/0024) -- but isolating learning rate for the *simple* reward (EXP-0023) surfaced something unexpected: a from-scratch run at a low learning rate reached a substantially better velocity-tracking optimum (`dist_ratio=0.848`) than any standard-learning-rate run ever had, with a real but separate stepping-quality regression (a small, rapid shuffle instead of a stride). Applying the project's own proven fine-tuning recipe (warm-start at low LR to introduce `feet_air_time_bonus`) to *that* better base, instead of to the original one, compounded rather than traded off (EXP-0025/0026): the resulting checkpoint kept the superior tracking, regained a real stride matching the project's best, and even improved heading control.

The **current canonical baseline** (`exp0026_air_time_on_exp0023_long`'s evaluated-and-selected 2M-step fine-tuning checkpoint) beats every prior checkpoint in the project on nearly every walking-quality metric simultaneously: **0/20 falls**, 20.0s mean survival, **0.036 m/s velocity error** (best of the project, a 41% improvement over the previous canonical), 4.28m mean distance traveled, **0.918 distance ratio** (best of the project by a wide margin, previous canonical: 0.709), yaw drift down to 17.6 degrees/10s in the full evaluation (best of any real-walking checkpoint) and 14.1 degrees/10s in a controlled straight-line test, and a **60ms median swing duration matching the longest verified stride in the project**. One narrow, honestly-reported gap remains: a different checkpoint (`exp0019_heading_hold`) still holds the single best controlled-heading measurement (-7.5 deg/10s). Full resolution: `docs/DECISIONS.md` ADR-011, `experiments/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` EXP-0025/EXP-0026.

## Observation/action definition
See `docs/RL_PIPELINE.md` "Observation vector" / "Action vector" for the full field-by-field table. Summary:
- **Observation** (44-dim): leg joint position (relative to default pose, 12) + leg joint velocity (12) + base angular velocity, body frame (3) + projected gravity, body frame (3) + commanded (vx, vy) (2) + previous action (12). Matches the challenge's listed fields; base *linear* velocity is deliberately excluded (privileged/hard to estimate on real hardware, following the sim2real papers the challenge points to).
- **Action** (12-dim, `[-1,1]`): per-leg-joint position-target delta from a default crouch pose, scaled by 0.25 rad and clipped to joint limits, converted to torque by a PD law (`control/pd_controller.py`) with per-joint-group gains.

## Reward function and rationale
Starts from exactly the challenge's stated minimum, plus additions made after specific, measured gait-quality failures (`rewards/walking_reward.py`, `docs/DECISIONS.md` ADR-002/ADR-005/ADR-007). The **canonical baseline's** reward (`config/exp0012_air_time.yaml`, unchanged since EXP-0012 -- used by `exp0017` and, via fine-tuning onto a different, better base checkpoint, the current canonical `exp0026`):

```
reward = 1.0  * exp(-||v_cmd_xy - v_base_xy||^2 / 0.25^2)   # tracking_lin_vel   (challenge minimum)
       + 0.5  * 1.0                                          # alive_bonus        (challenge minimum)
       - 0.0002 * sum(torque^2)                              # torque_penalty     (challenge minimum)
       - 0.1  * base_ang_vel_z^2                              # ang_vel_penalty    (added: F-0003, straight-line walking)
       - 0.01 * sum((action - prev_action)^2)                 # action_rate_penalty(added: F-0003, step smoothness)
       + 3.0  * (swing_time - 0.2) at each foot's touchdown    # feet_air_time_bonus(added: F-0004, stride duration)
```

No pose-imitation, orientation, or foot-slip terms were added -- not justified by any measured failure (CLAUDE.md: "add a term only after naming a measured failure it is meant to fix"). Every reward change actually tried is on record, including several rejected ones: lower `alive_bonus` alone and combined with `target_kl` (EXP-0007/0008, F-0002) made things worse; a first attempt at `ang_vel_penalty` (weight 1.0, EXP-0010) collapsed the policy to standing still, caught and corrected (F-0003); `feet_air_time_bonus` trained *from scratch* (EXP-0012) got gamed into a skating gait, and a complementary penalty to close that loophole (EXP-0013) caused a 100% fall rate (F-0004). The `feet_air_time_bonus` term above is unmodified from those failed attempts -- what changed was *how* it was introduced to training: fine-tuned from an already-good checkpoint at a reduced learning rate (`training/train.py --init-from ... --init-learning-rate 5e-5`) rather than trained from scratch.

**How the current canonical reached a checkpoint that dominates the old Pareto surface**: `exp0026` uses this exact reward config, unmodified -- what changed is the *starting checkpoint* it was fine-tuned from. Every earlier checkpoint (`exp0016`-`exp0019`) was fine-tuned from `exp0011`, a from-scratch base gait trained at the standard PPO learning rate (3e-4). `exp0023` (`config/exp0023_low_lr_from_scratch.yaml`, the challenge's original minimal reward -- no stepping/heading terms -- trained from scratch at a *lower* learning rate, 5e-5) reached a substantially better velocity-tracking optimum than `exp0011` ever did, with a real but separate stepping-quality regression (nothing in that minimal reward asks for a longer stride). Fine-tuning this canonical config onto `exp0023`'s checkpoint instead of `exp0011`'s (`experiments/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` EXP-0025/EXP-0026) inherited the better tracking and regained a real stride on top, rather than trading one for the other -- confirming the earlier Pareto surface was a property of the specific base gait every prior checkpoint descended from, not the reward family itself (`docs/DECISIONS.md` ADR-011).

Two documented **alternative** variants remain relative to that resolved surface: `config/exp0018_air_time_v3.yaml` doubles `feet_air_time_bonus` to 6.0 on top of the `exp0011` lineage (60ms median swing, matched by the new canonical, but worse tracking and heading than either); `config/exp0019_heading_hold.yaml` adds `heading_deviation_penalty=0.5` on top of that -- still holding the single best *controlled-heading* measurement of any checkpoint in the project (-7.5 deg/10s, vs. the new canonical's -14.1), the one axis it does not win outright, though the new canonical still beats it on tracking, full-eval heading, and falls. A direct attempt to close that one gap by fine-tuning `heading_deviation_penalty` onto the new canonical itself (EXP-0027/0028) was not promoted -- a striking near-miss checkpoint tied `exp0019`'s heading while beating canonical on tracking, but showed a persistent, weight-independent fall regression, revealing the new canonical sits at a fragile, narrow training optimum. `config/exp0021_dr_on_canonical.yaml` (reward unchanged, reused for both) adds a physics-robustness axis (friction/mass/motor-strength/pushes) -- fine-tuned this time onto the new canonical checkpoint instead of `exp0017` (`exp0029_dr_on_exp0026`, superseding the original `exp0021_dr_on_canonical`), falls under randomized physics drop 14/20 -> 4/20 relative to the correct control (a 71% reduction, stronger than the original `exp0021` result) at a real clean-physics tracking cost. A from-scratch attempt to find one checkpoint that dominates without any fine-tuning at all (EXP-0022/0024: all reward terms present from step 0, at two different learning rates) failed decisively both times -- neither ever learned to walk, confirming warm-starting from an already-competent policy is necessary, not just convenient, for this reward's complexity (`docs/FAILURE_ANALYSIS.md` F-0002 third/fourth occurrences).

## Deterministic evaluation
Status: done.
Protocol: `evaluation/evaluate.py`, 20 fixed seeds (1000-1019, disjoint from all training seeds), `model.predict(obs, deterministic=True)`, one episode per seed up to the 20s/1000-step truncation limit. Reports per-episode and aggregate: episode length, total reward, mean velocity-tracking error, mean torque cost, termination reason, final pelvis height, distance traveled and distance ratio (only averaged over episodes long enough to be meaningful -- ADR-003 addendum), and **yaw drift per 10s** (added for F-0003, catches heading instability that every other metric here misses). `evaluation/evaluate_checkpoints.py` extends this across every checkpoint of a run. `diagnostics/gait_diagnostic.py` runs a controlled single-command test (trajectory, foot-height, foot-contact plots, and -- used specifically to verify the stride-duration claims -- direct swing/stance-time measurement via `envs/contacts.py`) for a deeper look at heading and stepping quality specifically. `results/compare.py` tabulates every evaluated run side by side.

## Limitations
- Trained on CPU only; no GPU convergence run (explicitly named an "optional open challenge" in the source document, not required here).
- **Stride duration tripled from the original baseline (20ms -> 60ms median), and the current canonical baseline reaches this 60ms figure directly** (a plausible fully-deliberate human-scale stride would be 200-300ms, so this remains short of that, but it is the longest verified in the project, not a compromise). This took two rounds of investigation. Two from-scratch fixes were tried first and both failed instructively (F-0004): rewarding longer swings alone got gamed into a skating gait that avoids lifting its feet at all; closing that loophole with a competing penalty produced a 100% fall rate. What worked (EXP-0016/0017) was fine-tuning the same reward term from an already-good checkpoint at a low learning rate rather than training it from scratch, first reaching 0/20 falls at a 40ms median swing (docs/DECISIONS.md ADR-007/ADR-008). Pushing further by doubling the reward weight (EXP-0018) increased swing duration to 60ms but cost 23% of `dist_ratio` and worsened heading control -- a real three-way Pareto trade-off across tracking precision, stride length, and heading control that persisted through several more experiments (ADR-009/ADR-010) and looked like a property of the reward family itself. It was not: **the trade-off was resolved (ADR-011)** once the same stepping-quality fine-tune was applied to a better *base* gait (a from-scratch run at a lower learning rate, EXP-0023, which reached better tracking than any earlier base ever had) instead of to the original one -- the result (`exp0026`, current canonical) matches the 60ms stride while beating the tracking-precision and heading numbers of every earlier checkpoint. One narrow gap remains: `exp0019`'s controlled-heading measurement (-7.5 deg/10s) is still the single best in the project, better than the new canonical's -14.1 -- an honestly-reported residual, not a trade-off requiring a choice of checkpoint for any other purpose.
- **Commanded-speed-dependent falls, reliable in every prior baseline at speeds above ~0.4-0.5 m/s, were resolved in the then-canonical `exp0017` (0/20 falls, re-verified at the per-seed level against the exact seeds that used to fail) -- but the failure mode shifted rather than vanished.** The same fastest-command episodes survived the full episode but walked a curve instead: worst-in-run `distance_ratio` and largest heading drift, consistent with high commanded speed stressing heading control specifically rather than balance. This was measured against `exp0017`; re-verifying against the current canonical (`exp0026`) has not yet been done. See docs/FAILURE_ANALYSIS.md F-0002 second resolution.
- The canonical baseline covers ~92% of the commanded distance on average -- a real, repeatedly-improved walk, well past the challenge's own framing of "stumbling forward" as sufficient.
- **Domain randomization (Phase 8) is implemented, verified, and shows a genuine, meaningful robustness gain, kept as a separate variant, not a replacement.** `envs/domain_randomization.py` randomizes friction/mass/motor-strength per episode and applies periodic pushes. A first attempt (EXP-0014, 10M timesteps) underperformed even a policy trained with *no* randomization; doubling the budget (EXP-0015, 20M timesteps) confirmed this was simply undertraining (the same lesson that fixed the base gait, ADR-004). Re-running the identical recipe via warm-start fine-tuning onto the then-canonical `exp0017` (EXP-0021) reproduced the trade-off on that better base gait (falls 14/20 -> 7/20, survival up 58%); re-running it again onto the *current* canonical `exp0026` (EXP-0029, after ADR-011) produced an even stronger result -- falls under randomized physics drop 14/20 -> 4/20 (71% relative reduction) and survival nearly doubles (10.2s -> 18.8s), at a real clean-physics cost (`dist_ratio` 0.918 -> 0.643) that still leaves this DR checkpoint tracking better under clean physics than the *old* canonical `exp0017` ever did. Available at `experiments/runs/exp0029_dr_on_exp0026/best_checkpoint_6M.zip` (supersedes `exp0021`, which supersedes `exp0015`, both kept for history); see docs/DECISIONS.md ADR-006.
- No foot-contact-state *observation* (used only for one-off diagnostic analysis and the stride-duration reward, not fed to the policy directly) or foot-slip reward term; not yet needed by any measured failure beyond what F-0003/F-0004 already addressed.
- Upper body (waist/arms) is not RL-controlled; a real walking gait would eventually use arm swing for balance, which this project does not attempt.
- Wall-clock training cost for the same 10M-timestep budget varied from ~23 minutes to ~3.5 hours across runs in this session, apparently due to machine load/thermal effects from many consecutive long runs rather than the code itself -- budget time generously for reproduction on similar hardware.
- **Methodological limitations we found and fixed during this project** (all now standing practices, not one-off fixes): (1) total reward and episode survival are not sufficient evidence that a policy is walking (ADR-003) -- always check distance traveled; (2) a naive distance-ratio can itself be misleading for short/failed episodes (ADR-003 addendum) -- only average it over long-enough episodes; (3) a reward-shaping weight chosen by "similar order of magnitude to another term" can be wildly wrong -- measure what the term actually costs the behavior you don't want to destroy before trusting a guess (ADR-005); (4) measuring a new reward term in isolation does not validate how it *combines* with another new term -- an interacting pair can fail in ways neither term's individual calibration predicts (F-0004); (5) before concluding any technique (a reward term, domain randomization) doesn't work, test whether it was simply undertrained -- the single highest-leverage lever in this entire project (ADR-004, ADR-006); (6) a reward term that's exploitable when trained from scratch can work correctly when introduced via fine-tuning from an established policy instead -- the failure mode was about *how* the reward was introduced, not only its design (ADR-007); (7) an aggregate metric improving does not mean every underlying mechanism it depends on is still improving -- "policy still getting better" and "stride duration still increasing" turned out to be independent facts that only diverged once measured directly (ADR-008); (8) an instantaneous-magnitude penalty (rotation rate squared) is not automatically a good proxy for a cumulative/directional failure (net heading drift) -- a term that penalizes the actual quantity of interest can outperform one that only correlates with it (ADR-010); (9) a richer, multi-term reward that trains fine via sequential low-LR fine-tuning can be effectively untrainable from scratch -- presenting every term at once to a randomly-initialized policy can make "stand still and collect the survival bonus" such a strongly-attracting local optimum that even a noisy, high-KL, unconstrained PPO process never escapes it in 20M timesteps, even though the identical reward is easy to reach incrementally from an already-competent walker (EXP-0022, docs/FAILURE_ANALYSIS.md F-0002 third occurrence) -- curriculum order/starting point can matter as much as reward-shape correctness once a reward has several interacting terms; (10) a smooth, non-oscillating training curve is not the same claim as "found a good gait" -- lowering the learning rate alone (no warm start) does avoid the from-scratch PPO instability and can reach excellent aggregate tracking metrics, but the resulting policy can still take small, high-frequency, low-amplitude steps if nothing in the reward asks for a longer stride (EXP-0023: best `dist_ratio` in the project, 1.2cm foot lift -- a regression only the controlled gait diagnostic caught, not any of the standard aggregate metrics); and, following directly from that, lowering the LR does not rescue a from-scratch run using the *richer* reward either -- it produced an even more complete "stand still" collapse than the standard-LR attempt (EXP-0024), showing the trap is caused by the reward's own local-optimum structure (every non-tracking term is trivially satisfied by not moving), not by the optimizer's learning rate -- across every LR tried, only starting from an already-walking policy avoids it (docs/FAILURE_ANALYSIS.md F-0002 fourth occurrence); (11) a fine-tuning recipe's ceiling is bounded by the base policy it starts from -- low-LR fine-tuning by design makes only small departures from its starting point, so the best it can do is shift the trade-off *between* properties the base already has, not exceed what the base does well. Improving the base-training technique itself (not just the fine-tuning recipe) turned out to be the actual missing lever: applying the exact same, unmodified stepping-quality fine-tune to a better base gait (found via lesson 10's low-LR-from-scratch technique) compounded rather than traded off, resolving a trade-off three prior ADRs had characterized as fundamental (EXP-0025/0026, docs/DECISIONS.md ADR-011).
- **A training-process observation, now confirmed and directly tested**: every from-scratch PPO run at the standard learning rate in this project shows a repeating climb-crash-recover reward-curve oscillation (high `approx_kl`/`clip_fraction`). Checkpoint selection via evaluation (`evaluation/evaluate_checkpoints.py`) compensates for this. Low-learning-rate fine-tuning (EXP-0016/0017, EXP-0025/0026) consistently avoids it, and a direct isolating test (EXP-0023: a from-scratch run, not a fine-tune, at the same low learning rate, minimal reward) confirmed learning rate alone is sufficient to avoid the oscillation, independent of warm-starting -- though a companion test (EXP-0024) showed low LR does *not* rescue a from-scratch run using a richer, multi-term reward, where a different failure mode (a "stand still" degenerate optimum) dominates regardless of LR. See docs/FAILURE_ANALYSIS.md F-0002 for the full progression.
