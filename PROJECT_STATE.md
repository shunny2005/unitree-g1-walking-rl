- **How the stepping-and-heading investigation unfolded (EXP-0010 through EXP-0020)**: two from-scratch attempts to reward longer swing phases both failed (skating exploit; then 100% falls fixing that exploit, F-0004). Warm-start fine-tuning (`training/train.py --init-from`/`--init-learning-rate`, a new capability) from an established policy at a low learning rate fixed this -- swing duration doubled with no exploit (EXP-0016), then tripled with continued budget reaching 0/20 falls (EXP-0017). Pushing the same lever harder (EXP-0018) revealed a Pareto trade-off: longer strides cost heading control. Measuring *why* (instantaneous rotation-rate-squared can't distinguish a small steady drift bias from harmless zero-mean oscillation) motivated a better-targeted `heading_deviation_penalty` term (penalizes net drift from episode-start heading directly), which recovered and exceeded the original heading quality while keeping the longer strides (EXP-0019) -- though still short of canonical's tracking precision. A budget-extension test (EXP-0020) confirmed this specific lineage has genuinely plateaued, not simply undertrained.

## Latest experiment
**EXP-0049 (2026-09-24): heading cost scales down with a smaller asymmetric-gain step, but the swing-duration benefit vanishes too -- closes the "asymmetric ankle gain" line for this session.** At left kp=75 (half of EXP-0048's 90), yaw drift dropped to ~10.0 deg/10s (from EXP-0048's 19.6-24.1) -- but medium-bucket left-foot swing duration also dropped back to essentially the canonical's own 106ms (102-107ms across all 4 checkpoints), not a proportional partial gain. The two effects don't move together: heading cost is roughly continuous in gain step size, swing-duration benefit behaves like a threshold effect. Not promoted. **This is the gait-quality program's planned stopping point for this session** -- see `docs/GAIT_QUALITY_PROGRAM_SUMMARY.md` for the full end-of-session synthesis (baseline vs. best result, every rejected experiment, remaining limitations, confidence levels, and 3 prioritized next experiments).

Immediately before this: **EXP-0048 (2026-09-24): does more control authority specifically for the left ankle close its persistent swing-duration gap? Confirmed yes, but with a severe side effect -- not promoted.** New capability added (`config.pd_gains_left`, per-leg PD gains) after finding the left foot's shorter swing is consistent across every checkpoint measured this session (4 independent training configs, never reversed) and confirming model physical symmetry (mass/inertia/forcerange/sagittal joint ranges all identical between legs) -- ruling out a mundane bug. Before that: **EXP-0044 (2026-09-23): gait-quality research program started.** New instrumentation (`docs/GAIT_QUALITY_OBJECTIVE.md`, `evaluation/gait_quality.py`, `evaluation/evaluate_gait_quality.py`) measures fluidity/symmetry/geometry/efficiency, none of which any prior evaluation tracked. Baseline (no training) ran it across a 7-bucket command grid on the canonical and surfaced two significant, previously-unknown findings: **F-0006** (55% fall rate on a pure-lateral command, in-range but never isolated before) and **F-0007** (realized swing duration ~1/3 of the gait clock's own scheduled window; peak clearance undershoots the reward's own 0.08m target; an untrained PD step-response probe found the ankle joint's convergence time is comparable to or slower than actual swing durations). See `docs/GAIT_QUALITY_OBJECTIVE.md`, `experiments/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` EXP-0044, `docs/FAILURE_ANALYSIS.md` F-0006/F-0007.

Immediately before this: **EXP-0043 (2026-09-23): confirmed the zero-command fix is durable, not just a lucky checkpoint (ADR-019).** `exp0042`'s own promotion left an explicit open question: its checkpoint trend was turbulent for 4 of 6 checkpoints, and only the final two (5M, 6M) were good -- not enough to know if the mechanism (training directly on zero commands) was actually self-reinforcing or just found a favorable window. EXP-0043 continued the identical training trajectory (warm-started from `exp0042`'s own 6M-step endpoint, same config/LR) for 6M more steps and re-ran the zero-command test at every new checkpoint. Result: all 6 new checkpoints (7M-12M) cleared the bar 4 of `exp0042`'s own checkpoints had missed, and the trend was net-*improving* -- ending at 2.1 deg/20s (fresh seeds: 1.7), the lowest zero-command spin measured anywhere in this project, with general mixed-command heading also improved further (17% lower than `exp0042`) and tracking/falls tied. **F-0005 is now considered well-resolved.** See `experiments/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` EXP-0043, `docs/FAILURE_ANALYSIS.md` F-0005 third resolution.

Immediately before this: **EXP-0042 (2026-09-22): trained directly on zero commands instead of only penalizing zero-command behavior (ADR-018).** ADR-017 flagged its own weakness: `exp0041`'s zero-command fix was a static reward-weight increase, never reinforced by the training distribution (`vx ~ U[0.0, 0.6]` almost never samples exactly 0), and its checkpoint trend showed the fix eroding with more fine-tuning. EXP-0042 added `command.zero_command_prob` (new config field, `envs/reset.sample_command`) so 15% of training episodes get an exact (0,0) command, and fine-tuned 6M more steps from `exp0041`@1M. The result was messier than hoped -- the checkpoint trend was turbulent for 4 of 6 checkpoints, missing the pre-registered "no checkpoint worse than 12 deg/20s" bar -- but the run's final two checkpoints (5M, 6M) were its best, and the 5M checkpoint, verified on disjoint seeds, beat `exp0041`@1M on both the specific zero-command failure and general mixed-command heading at no tracking cost. Promoted with the durability question about further training explicitly left open -- which EXP-0043 then answered. See `experiments/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` EXP-0042, `docs/FAILURE_ANALYSIS.md` F-0005 second resolution.

Before that: **EXP-0034 through EXP-0038 (2026-09-21): a research-driven lineage that produced a new canonical (ADR-012 to ADR-015).** Research into the official Unitree G1 code and Humanoid-Gym (R-0004) found the missing structure: a gait clock in the observation plus per-step contact rewards. EXP-0034 (reference weight) was ignored by the network; EXP-0035/0036 (weight 1.0, then 10M more steps) got the policy following the clock (81% adherence, median swing 60 -> 120 ms) but velocity error rose to 0.085. A measurement (R-0005) showed that error was mostly within-stride ripple, not mis-tracking (and corrected an earlier wrong claim about overshoot); Humanoid-Gym's steeper velocity reward closed it (EXP-0037, 0.041). A second measurement (R-0006) showed the remaining deficit was high-speed heading drift, a small steady yaw-rate bias hidden under a large in-stride oscillation, so the accumulated-heading penalty (not the reference's instantaneous yaw-rate reward) was used: EXP-0038 cut high-speed drift 43 -> 15 deg/10 s and was promoted after beating `exp0026` on three disjoint seed sets. Also built along the way: opt-in gait clock, three gait reward terms, function-preserving checkpoint expansion, `tracking_lin_vel_hard`, velocity-error decomposition, and speed-banded fresh-seed evaluation (99 tests). See `experiments/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` EXP-0034 to EXP-0038, `docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md` R-0004 to R-0006.

Before that: reward annealing (EXP-0032/0033) did not work -- it avoided the total standing-still collapse but ended in a skating gait -- closing the from-scratch route for this reward family. Before that: EXP-0025/0026 resolved the ADR-009/010 Pareto surface (ADR-011).

## Highest-value next step
Challenge's minimum bar has been exceeded by a very wide margin for a long time; everything below is optional. **Per `docs/GAIT_QUALITY_PROGRAM_SUMMARY.md`'s prioritized recommendation, the single highest-value next step is the clock-structure hypothesis test** (a from-scratch comparison swapping which foot the gait clock's `clock_features` references, to test whether that flips which foot gets the shorter swing) -- deliberately not started this session since it needs a from-scratch run (hours), not this session's ~10-minute bounded-fine-tune cadence. Second: recalibrate heading-control reward terms for an asymmetric-gain regime (EXP-0049 found the heading cost scales predictably while the swing benefit doesn't, making this the more tractable lever). Third: isolate F-0006's actual mechanism (ablate the ankle-gain and zero-command changes separately) and confirm its 10% fall rate on a larger seed set. Other open items, as of ADR-020 (the canonical is `exp0047`):
1. ~~Confirm durability of the zero-command fix past 6M steps.~~ -- done 2026-09-23 (EXP-0043, ADR-019). F-0005 is now considered well-resolved.
2. ~~Investigate F-0006 (lateral-command falls).~~ -- substantially improved as an incidental side effect of EXP-0047 (55% -> 10%, confirmed on fresh seeds), mechanism not isolated. See `docs/GAIT_QUALITY_PROGRAM_SUMMARY.md` recommendation #3.
3. Rebuild push-only domain-randomization robustness (`exp0039`'s approach) on `exp0047` and check whether it composes with the zero-command fix and the new ankle gains -- still not rebuilt past `exp0038` (see ADR-016 for the original push-vs-motor-strength ablation).
4. **More than one training seed.** Every run used seed 0, for the entire project including every gait-quality experiment. The single most impactful unverified assumption underlying every finding in this program.
5. **The fixed 0.8 s gait period.** The clock runs at one period at every speed (the reference behavior); a speed-dependent period is untried -- F-0007 gives a concrete data point (realized swing duration still well under the clock's own 360ms schedule even after EXP-0047's improvement) suggesting the current period may still not leave enough time even with the improved gains.
6. **The recurring low-command fall.** Seeds 1004/1005 (commands 0.15-0.18 m/s) caused falls in `baseline0001`, EXP-0027 and `exp0037`; the canonical does not fall on them, but the cause was never found.
7. **Asymmetric critic** (privileged base velocity/contact for the value function only), used by both reference implementations; not supported by stable-baselines3 without a custom policy, so untried (rejected as a larger change in ADR-012).

Resolved items from earlier cycles (kept for the record):
1. ~~Fine-tune `heading_deviation_penalty` onto the new canonical (`exp0026`)~~ -- tried 2026-09-19 (EXP-0027/0028), inconclusive/not promoted. At weight=2.0, the 1M checkpoint was a striking near-miss (`dist_ratio=0.996`, controlled heading -7.5 deg/10s tying `exp0019`'s best-in-project figure, real stride) but had 2/20 falls, and training destabilized further with more budget (10/20 falls by 3M). 2. ~~Re-run domain randomization on top of the new canonical `exp0026` reward~~ -- done 2026-09-19 (EXP-0029): an even stronger robustness gain than EXP-0021's own (falls 14/20 -> 4/20 under randomized physics vs. the correct control, a 71% relative reduction; survival nearly doubles). `exp0029_dr_on_exp0026` supersedes `exp0021` for this purpose. 3. ~~Re-verify the high-speed-command failure mode (F-0002) against the new canonical `exp0026`~~ -- done 2026-09-19, no training needed: 0/20 falls holds, and the `dist_ratio` degradation at high speed shrank substantially (63% relative drop under `exp0017` -> ~6% under `exp0026`), though heading still degrades disproportionately at high speed in both. 4. ~~Does more budget on the low-LR-from-scratch base training raise the tracking ceiling further?~~ -- done 2026-09-20 (EXP-0030): no. Continuing `exp0023`'s exact training for 10M more timesteps (20M total) never beat its original 8M-timestep peak (0.848) -- the trend settled into a noisier, *lower* plateau (~0.72-0.79) instead. Unlike the standard-LR case (EXP-0009, where more budget found new higher peaks), this low-LR lineage's peaks look like noisy fluctuations around a converged level, not an ascending trajectory. The ceiling is confirmed at `exp0023`'s original point; `exp0026`/`exp0029` (built on it) remain the best results. See `experiments/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` EXP-0030.
5. ~~Retry the heading fine-tune onto `exp0026` with a lower LR instead of a lower weight~~ -- done 2026-09-20 (EXP-0031). Confirmed the mechanism: falls stabilized to 0/20 from 2M through 3M timesteps (the first heading fine-tune onto `exp0026` to hold 0/20 at all), and `dist_ratio` reached 1.099 -- the best in the project. But `vel_err` regressed (0.036 -> 0.061) and the controlled-heading result was inconclusive/noisy across checkpoints -- the originally-targeted gap (matching `exp0019`'s -7.5 deg/10s) was not closed.
